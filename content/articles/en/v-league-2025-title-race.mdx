The **V.League 2025/26** season has reached its most dramatic chapter. With just eight rounds remaining, **Hanoi FC** and **Viettel FC** are locked in a title race that could go down to the final matchday. After 26 rounds, the capital club leads on 54 points — but Viettel are just two points behind on 52, with a superior head-to-head record that could prove decisive if the teams finish level on points.

This is not the first time these two giants have contested a championship, but the 2025/26 campaign feels different. Attendances are up across the league, television ratings have hit record highs, and both clubs have invested heavily in foreign signings and coaching staff over the winter transfer window.

## Hanoi FC: consistency under Chun Jae-ho

Hanoi FC appointed **Chun Jae-ho** in the summer of 2024 with a clear mandate: reclaim the V.League title after two seasons without the trophy. The South Korean coach has delivered exactly that kind of stability — his side has lost just three league games all season and went unbeaten through the entire first half of the campaign.

At the heart of their success is striker **Rafaelson**. The Brazilian forward arrived from the J2 League and has been nothing short of sensational, scoring **12 goals** and providing 5 assists in 24 appearances. His movement off the shoulder of centre-backs has given Hanoi a cutting edge that was missing in previous seasons.

Behind Rafaelson, midfielder **Đỗ Hùng Dũng** has orchestrated play from deep with his trademark vision and range of passing. The 31-year-old captain has started every league game and his experience in high-pressure situations — including AFC Champions League nights — has been invaluable for a squad that features several young domestic talents.

Defensively, Hanoi have conceded just 19 goals in 26 games, the best record in the division. Goalkeeper **Nguyễn Văn Toản** has kept 11 clean sheets, while centre-back pairing **Nguyễn Thành Chung** and **Bùi Hoàng Việt Anh** have formed one of the most reliable defensive units in Southeast Asian club football.

> "We take each match as a final. The players understand what is at stake — this city expects the championship." — **Chun Jae-ho, Hanoi FC head coach**

## Viettel FC: the army that refuses to surrender

If Hanoi represent consistency, **Viettel FC** embody resilience. The military club have been written off twice this season — after a slow start and again following a damaging home defeat to SHB Da Nang in March — yet they have always found a way back into contention.

Coach **Hoàng Anh Tuấn** has built a team that is tactically flexible and physically imposing. Viettel press high, win second balls, and transition quickly through wide players **Nguyễn Hoàng Đức** and **Phạm Tuấn Hải**, both of whom have been linked with moves abroad.

Striker **Ngo Hoàng Thắng** has scored 10 league goals and is particularly dangerous in the air — a weapon that could be crucial in the upcoming direct clash at Hàng Đẫy Stadium. Viettel's set-piece routines, coached meticulously by assistant **Trương Việt Hoàng**, have produced 8 goals this season, the most in the league.

Viettel's remaining schedule is arguably kinder than Hanoi's. They face only one other top-four side in their last eight games, while Hanoi must travel to Becamex Binh Duong and host a resurgent Ho Chi Minh City FC side that has won six of their last seven.

## The top-four battle: HAGL, Becamex and Da Nang

While the title race captures headlines, the fight for **AFC Champions League Two** qualification is equally compelling. **Hoang Anh Gia Lai (HAGL)** sit third on 47 points, five behind Viettel, and have been the season's surprise package under young coach **Lê Huỳnh Đức**. Their academy graduates — including midfielder **Nguyễn Công Phượng** in a deeper role — have flourished in a system built on quick passing and aggressive pressing.

**Becamex Binh Duong**, fourth on 44 points, have relied on the goals of **Tiago Leonardo** (14 league goals) and the creativity of **Tô Văn Vũ**. A win against Hanoi in round 28 could reshape the entire top of the table.

**SHB Da Nang**, fifth on 42 points, are the dark horses. Their 2-1 victory over Viettel in March proved they can beat anyone on their day, and with home form among the best in the league, they cannot be discounted from the top-four race either.

## Fan atmosphere and media coverage

V.League 2025/26 has captured the Vietnamese public imagination in a way few seasons have. Social media engagement is up 40% year-on-year, and the Viettel-Hanoi derby trended nationally on matchdays. Pundits on **VTV** and **K+** have dedicated weekly segments to the title race, while podcasts and fan channels are producing daily analysis content.

For neutrals, the hope is that the race goes to the final day. For Hanoi and Viettel supporters, the next eight weeks will feel like a lifetime — but that is exactly what makes football worth watching.
